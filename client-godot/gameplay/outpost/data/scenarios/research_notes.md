# Isles of Mythos — Scenario research notes (2026-11-21)

Grounding for the scenario library. Every scenario's `history_note` should trace back to one of these facts.

## 1. Real castaways
- **Alexander Selkirk, Juan Fernández (1704–1709).** 4 yrs 4 months. Lived on feral goats (ran them down on foot), shellfish, turtles, sea lions, wild turnips/cabbage palm. Goatskin clothes. Rats gnawed his feet at night → he tamed feral cats; tamed kid goats for milk/company. Read the Bible aloud to keep his speech; two huts (sleep + cook). Hid from Spanish ships (they'd enslave him), waited for English. Sources: RMG https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/curatorial/dealing-self-isolation-desert-island-guide-real-robinson-crusoe ; DNB https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography%2C_1885-1900/Selkirk%2C_Alexander ; Pirate Surgeon's Journal https://www.piratesurgeon.com/pages/surgeon_pages/goats_in_diet4.html
- **Pedro Serrano, Serrana Bank (1520s, ~8 yrs; via Garcilaso de la Vega 1609).** No water/wood on the cay. Ate washed-up cockles/shrimp raw; flipped sea turtles onto their backs, drank blood when no rain; sun-dried strips; used shells as rain catchers (up to 12 gallons) and as a fire hut. Dove for two stones to strike sparks with his knife, shirt thread as tinder; seaweed, shells and wreck planks as fuel. Provisions rotted in heat/rain; sunburn drove him into the sea. Ships passed and ignored his smoke for fear of the shoals. A second castaway arrived; they quarrelled, then reconciled. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Serrano_(sailor) ; https://www.djo.org.uk/all-the-year-round/volume-v/page-66.html ; Frontiers 2019 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00817/full
- **Tromelin (L'Utile, 1761–1776).** French crew built boat *La Providence* from the wreck in 2 months, sailed off promising to return for ~60–80 Malagasy slaves. 15 years. Dug a well (walked 750 m for water), stored water in salvaged lead basins, triton shell as ladle. Ate sooty terns (colonies of tens of thousands), green turtles, reef fish (jacks, groupers, triggerfish) by line, spear and tide-pool gathering. Kept one fire alive 15 years with wreck timber + driftwood + the island's single shrub; flint strikers worn to nothing. Built 1.5 m thick coral/beachrock walls with backs to the trade wind after a storm. Rescue boats smashed on the reef twice; a group left on a raft with a **sail of woven feathers**, never seen again. 7 women + a baby rescued. Sources: Archaeology Mag 2014 https://archaeology.org/issues/september-october-2014/features/tromelin-island-castaways/ ; INRAP https://magazine.inrap.fr/magazine/tromelin/vivre-tromelin/boire-et-manger and /sabriter ; Int J Osteoarchaeol https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/oa.2763
- **Ada Blackjack, Wrangel Island (1921–1923).** Relief ship failed; 3 men walked off over ice, never seen again. Nursed Lorne Knight through scurvy until he died; barricaded his body with boxes against animals. Taught herself to shoot, trap foxes, hunt birds, collect eggs; built a stove from kerosene cans, a lookout platform on the tent, a skin boat. Alone with the cat Vic. Fog lifted → ship. Sources: NPS https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/ada-blackjack-stranded-on-wrangel-island.htm ; Dartmouth https://www.library.dartmouth.edu/digital/digital-collections/collating-wrangel-island/ada-blackjack-diary

## 2. Survival science (applied to tropical islands)
- Rule of threes: ~3 hours exposure, ~3 days without water, ~3 weeks without food. Water and shade come before food.
- **Never drink seawater**: kidneys can't excrete the salt; worsens dehydration (NOAA https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/drinksw.html). Options: rain catchment (sails, shells, leaves), green coconut water (fine in moderation; too much = laxative), solar still, digging behind the dune line (brackish lens), turtle blood (Serrano).
- **Manchineel** ("little apple of death"): sweet fruit causes blistering, vomiting, throat swelling; sap blisters skin; even rain dripping off it burns; smoke damages eyes (WebMD https://www.webmd.com/first-aid/manchineel-poisoning ; ACEP https://www.acepnow.com/article/toxicology-answer-the-manchineel-tree/).
- **Ciguatera**: big reef predators (grouper, snapper, barracuda, moray) carry toxin; vomiting, tingling, hot-cold reversal; not destroyed by cooking (Cleveland Clinic https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/ciguatera). Rule: eat small reef fish.
- Spoilage: meat rots in a day in tropical heat (Serrano); smoke/dry or salt it. Raw shellfish = gut sickness risk.
- Injuries: coral cuts infect fast; stonefish/lionfish/urchin stings (hot water helps); moray bites (NOAA reef hazards https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/45697). Sunburn and heatstroke; hypothermia at night when wet.
- Scurvy after weeks without fresh greens/fruit (Ada Blackjack; sailors generally).
- Fire: friction (bow drill) is hard in humid air; flint + steel / knife + quartz; dry tinder (coconut husk fibre, shirt thread, bird down); keep a fire alive rather than relight (Tromelin).
- Signalling: three fires in a triangle, green leaves for white smoke, mirror flash; ships avoid shoals (Serrano was ignored).
- Psychology: routines, talking aloud, keeping a tally/journal, faith; companion animals (Selkirk's cats, Ada's cat Vic) reduce despair; false hope from passing ships; conflict with a second survivor (Serrano).

## 3. Golden Age pirate life (c. 1690–1726)
- **Havens**: Nassau (pirate republic ~1713–1718, Woodes Rogers' pardon 1718), Tortuga, Port Royal (destroyed by earthquake 1692), Île Sainte-Marie off Madagascar (Adam Baldridge's trading post from 1690: sold cattle to careening crews for guns, powder, iron; 1697 islanders rose and killed ~30 pirates over stolen cattle and abuse; Condent's *Fiery Dragon* scuttled in the bay 1721 as a pardon condition; ships deliberately sunk as a harbour barrier). Sources: https://sailingtheblackflag.com/places/ile-sainte-marie/ ; https://archeologiedelapiraterie.fr/en/projet/ile-sainte-marie/introduction ; https://sailingtheblackflag.com/themes/pirate-ports-and-informal-economies/
- **The haven chain**: haven, victualler, receiver (fence), protector (bribed officials).
- **Careening**: teredo worm and weed slow the hull; ship grounded at high tide on a sloping beach, heeled over, scraped, plugged and coated with tar/tallow/turtle fat. Crew is defenceless while careening; guns often landed in a beach battery. Pirate "island haunts" chosen for careening beaches and turtling (JMU thesis https://commons.lib.jmu.edu/master201019/531).
- **Articles (Bartholomew Roberts, via Johnson 1724)**: vote on affairs; equal share of fresh food and liquor; defrauding the company → marooning with a gun, shot, bottle of water, bottle of powder; theft between mates → slit ears and nose, put ashore somewhere hard; no gambling for money; lights out at 8; weapons kept clean; desertion → death or marooning; quarrels settled ashore by pistol then cutlass, first blood wins; injury compensation (800 dollars for a limb); captain & quartermaster 2 shares. Sources: https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/captain-charles-johnson/a-general-history-of-the-pirates/text/chapter-11 ; https://sailingtheblackflag.com/themes/pirate-justice-and-discipline/
- **Tactics**: false flags (fly friendly colours, raise the black flag at close range); intimidation to make victims strike without a fight (most prizes surrendered); night and dawn boarding from boats with muffled oars; ambush in narrow channels and behind headlands; grapeshot to clear decks; taking hostages and ransom (Roberts used a captured master to identify the richest Portuguese ship); torture to find hidden valuables; recruiting/forcing skilled men (carpenters, surgeons); deserters giving intelligence (a deserter told Roberts how HMS *Swallow* sailed best).
- **Camp life**: tents of sails, rum, turtle and goat barbecues ("boucan"), gambling ashore, fights, disease (fever killed most of the Susanna's crew at St Mary's), trading with locals, quartermaster as camp judge, lookouts on headlands.

## 4. Tropical island wildlife (game-relevant behaviour)
- **Feral pigs/boar**: introduced by sailors; root up ground, eat crab juveniles and eggs; charge when cornered or with piglets; tusks cause deep leg wounds (SPREP https://library.sprep.org/sites/default/files/operational-plan-feral-pig-management.pdf).
- **Feral goats**: left by sailors as living larders (Juan Fernández); wary, climb cliffs; kids tameable (Selkirk).
- **Coconut crab** (*Birgus latro*): nocturnal, burrows, claws up to ~3,300 N, climbs palms, scavenges and steals shiny objects; meat prized; overharvested (Current Biology https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822%2817%2931462-8 ; RBZ 2022 https://doi.org/10.26107/rbz-2022-0001). Land crabs migrate en masse to spawn.
- **Snakes**: tree boas, sea kraits (venomous but placid, come ashore at night).
- **Monkeys/macaques**: raid camps, steal food and shiny tools, troop alarm calls warn of predators.
- **Seabirds**: tern and booby colonies (eggs and meat at Tromelin), frigatebirds steal fish; guano; gulls signal fish schools and land.
- **Sea turtles**: nest at night; flipped on their backs to catch (Serrano); shells as vessels.
- **Sharks**: reef sharks drawn by blood/thrashing in the shallows; tiger sharks at dusk.
- **Rays**: stingrays buried in sand — shuffle feet; barb wounds.
- **Moray eels**: bite when hands go into crevices; carry ciguatera.
- **Insects**: mosquitoes (fever), sandflies at dusk, centipedes in bedding, fire ants.
- **Parrots**: mimic speech, sold in havens.
